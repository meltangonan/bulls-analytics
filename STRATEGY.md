# @chicagobullsdata Strategy

## Target problem

An everyday Bulls fan who wants league-quality data storytelling *specifically about the Bulls* has
nowhere to go. The high-craft NBA viz accounts (Basketball University, Kirk Goldsberry, Owen
Phillips) are all league-wide; the Bulls-specific accounts are fan/meme/occasional-stat pages with no
craft. That single-team-plus-craft intersection is empty for *every* team — not because it was tried
and failed, but because nobody with the craft has bothered.

## Our approach

Win on craft: every Bulls post should look like it belongs next to the best NBA viz accounts, but the
bar is *pretty **and** instantly legible to a normal Bulls fan*, not just pretty. That combination
rules out both the ugly stat-dumps the fan pages post and the beautiful-but-inscrutable charts only
stats people decode, so the account owns the empty single-team-craft corner by being eye candy a
casual fan actually gets.

**One-liner:** The top account in the space — but for data. *Chicago Bulls, charted.*

## Who it's for

**Primary:** The everyday Bulls fan who'd never call themselves a stats person. They meet the work
mid-scroll and are hiring the account to show them a Bulls stat they didn't know, as a chart clean
enough to get in two seconds and pretty enough to stop the scroll ("huh, didn't know that").

**Secondary (a bonus, not the design target):** Data-literate hoops nerds and the "top guys" in the
space, whose reposts and respect are gravy.

**Decision rule:** when a detail would help a nerd but confuse a casual fan, the casual fan wins.

**Belief ladder:** this is about *my* team → I understood it instantly → that was genuinely new to me
→ they do this all the time → follow.

**Primary CTA is the follow; the secondary is the share** — sending the post to a friend or group
chat is the engine that puts posts in front of non-followers.

## Key metrics

- **Shares / sends per post** — someone sent your chart to a friend or dropped it in a group chat.
  The belonging signal, chosen over saves on purpose: sharing is social, saving is solitary.
  *Instagram Insights, weekly.*
- **Engagement rate per post** — likes + saves + shares + comments relative to reach. Do the
  followers you *have* actually care? A rate, so it can regress even as followers grow. *Instagram
  Insights, weekly.*
- **Community recognition** — reposts, collabs, shoutouts, and follows from other Bulls pages and the
  "top guys." The metric closest to the actual goal. *Qualitative running note, moves over months.*

Treat published work as a learning loop, not a verdict on one post.

## Tracks

**Craft & the format library** — the reusable visual grammar (boards, tables, grids, shared-scale
comparisons, the occasional court map) plus the polish that clears the top-tier bar. The craft bet
*is* this track. Settled with the debut post (2026-07-09): the account speaks "fan in the stands" on
the graphic, with names and context in the caption. The concrete system lives in `DESIGN.md`.

**The idea-to-post engine** — the steady supply of genuinely surprising Bulls angles, posted *often*.
Craft needs substance to apply it to, and tempo is itself a top growth lever (per Owen Phillips,
consistency is the key to success). The editorial POV lives in `bulls-content-playbook.html`; the
idea shelf and post log live in the Notion `chicagobullsdata posts` database.

**Community & distribution** — belonging is the goal and the #1 no-budget growth lever. A niche
account grows by *borrowing* other pages' audiences, not by outspending anyone.

## Engagement principles

Participate before you promote. Leave occasional, specific comments that add a useful observation, a
good question, or the right kind of humor for that post. Don't mass-engage, link-drop, or make every
interaction about this account.

Build familiarity through repeated genuine participation, then make a warm, no-pressure introduction
to high-fit peer pages. A repost or collaboration follows a relevant original post and an existing
relationship — it's an endorsement to earn, not a cold ask.

Distribution advice stays small and specific to the post. Generic engagement checklists are not a
strategy.

## Not working on

- Chasing raw follower count as the headline goal — belonging beats scale.
- League-wide coverage — single-team focus is the whole point; breadth dilutes the corner.
- Fan-page content (memes, stories, hot takes) — that lane is taken, and it isn't the craft bet.
- Designing charts for the data nerds first — see the decision rule.
- Social-media-persona copy: manufactured hooks, slang, rhetorical questions, bait.
