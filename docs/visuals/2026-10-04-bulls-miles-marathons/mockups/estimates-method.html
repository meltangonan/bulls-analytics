How we estimated the miles

Measured since 2013-14, estimated from minutes before that

1What is measured

Since 2013-14, cameras in every NBA arena track how far each player moves during games. Those miles are real measurements: Zach LaVine's 1,093 miles as a Bull are counted, not estimated.

2How we estimated earlier seasons

Before tracking, we only know minutes. Miles follow minutes closely, so for each Bulls season before 2013-14:

Estimated miles = minutes played × miles per minute

Players with enough tracked Bulls minutes (Hinrich, Noah, Rose) use their own measured pace. Everyone else uses the Bulls average from 2013-14 to 2016-17: 0.070 miles per minute, about 3.4 miles per 48-minute game.

Example: Michael Jordan
43,364 Bulls minutes, regular season and playoffs
43,364 × 0.0703 = 3,048 miles ÷ 26.2 = 116 marathons

3How sure we are

Minutes predict miles well: using a player's own pace, a tracked season lands within about 2% of the measured miles. The ranges show a slower and a faster pace, and they can't capture how the speed of play changed between eras.

*Estimates rank the order well, since minutes are exact; treat individual totals as approximate.
